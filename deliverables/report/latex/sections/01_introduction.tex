\section{Introduction and Educational Motivation}
\label{sec:intro}

\textbf{Can a clever model predict tomorrow's price better than doing nothing?}
Short-horizon prices are close to a random walk \citep{fama1970efficient}, so the
rule \textbf{\emph{tomorrow's price equals today's}} is hard to beat. Introductory
examples where better models visibly win can hide two questions:
\emph{what did the model learn}, and \emph{against what baseline}?

We answer in four steps:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Build a fair test} (Sections~\ref{sec:data}--\ref{sec:tuning}).
  \item \textbf{Try to break it} for leaks (Section~\ref{sec:features}).
  \item \textbf{Face the baseline} (Sections~\ref{sec:ablation}--\ref{sec:dl}).
  \item \textbf{Teach the answer} (Section~\ref{sec:education}).
\end{enumerate}

We compare Apple (AAPL) and Bitcoin (BTC). Our \textbf{audience} is advanced
secondary-school students, pre-university learners and university students without
a machine-learning major.
