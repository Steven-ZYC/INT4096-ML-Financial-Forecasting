\section{Model Selection and Baselines}
\label{sec:models}

\textbf{Naive persistence}, $\hat{C}_{t+1} = C_t$, is the mandatory baseline
\citep{makridakis2018m4,hyndman2021fpp3}. Table~\ref{tab:models} shows why each
model is included.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{The baseline and five models.}
\label{tab:models}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}L{0.30\linewidth} L{0.37\linewidth} L{0.26\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Model} & \textbf{How it predicts} & \textbf{Role here} \\
\midrule
Naive persistence & copies today's close & the yardstick \\
Linear baseline & weighted sum, small L2 penalty & simplest learned model \\
Ridge \citep{hastie2009elements} & weighted sum, wider penalty search & does regularisation help? \\
SVR, RBF kernel \citep{drucker1997support} & similarity to training examples & scale-sensitive case \\
Random forest \citep{breiman2001random} & average of independently grown trees & scale-invariant control \\
Hist.\ gradient boosting \citep{friedman2001gradient} & trees added in sequence to fix errors & boosting versus bagging \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

All models share \repodir{src/int4096/models}{one code path} and the same
features. Gradient boosting stops early using a random 10\% of its training rows.
Neural networks stay outside the main comparison because the handout discourages
deep learning (see Section~\ref{sec:dl}).
