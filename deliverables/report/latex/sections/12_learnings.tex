\section{Challenges and Team Learnings}
\label{sec:learnings}

The first difficulty was representational. Initial runs reported skill near
$-1800\%$, which looked like a modelling failure and was not: every feature was
scale free, so no model could recover a price level and all collapsed towards the
training mean. The fix was recorded as a decision rather than applied silently.

The second was the boundary purge, which no inspection would have found; it took an
auditor tracing a label to a specific test-set price. Relatedly, our first leakage
tests passed while a one-bar forward shift was present: a green suite only means
something once it has been shown able to fail.

The third was fairness: one construction path, one budget and identical columns
cost more effort than the models, and make the comparison meaningful. Each family also had one accountable
owner, reviewed by an agent that had not written the code --- which is why two
high-severity defects were found before submission.
