Can a model beat tomorrow equals today?

We forecast the next daily close of Apple and Bitcoin with five classical machine-learning models, under one leakage-safe protocol, and compared every one with a rule that just copies today's price. This page walks through each step with the real data.

01

Data

Which data, from where, and why these two markets?

Two public daily price histories, downloaded once, saved unchanged and fingerprinted with SHA-256. Every run re-checks the fingerprint, so every number traces back to the same bytes.

We picked them because they differ in almost every way that matters for a forecaster:

A finding that holds on both structures is about the method, not one market. Because the price scales differ by over a hundred times, the two are compared only with unit-free scores.

02

Selection and split

Which rows qualify, and how are they cut in time?

Every row has to meet four conditions:

  • Window: five years from 28 Oct 2021, after Apple's 2020 split, so raw prices have no split jumps.
  • Raw close, not adjusted close: adjustments are revised after the fact and would leak the future.
  • Quality gate: no duplicate dates, missing values, non-positive prices, high below low, close outside the day's range or negative volume. A failure stops the pipeline; nothing is quietly repaired.
  • Complete history: drop the first 30 rows (rolling windows not yet full) and the last row (no next close to predict).

The rows are then split by date: 60% train, 20% validation, 20% test. One row is dropped at each boundary, because the label is the next close: without that, the last training answer would be the first validation price.

Tuning only ever sees the training block, through five expanding-window folds. The validation block is used once, for reporting. The test block stays sealed.

03

Features

What can the model see, and how is each number made?

Raw prices drift to levels the training years never contained, so a model fitted on prices cannot extrapolate. Each day is turned into ten ratios instead, all computed from that day and earlier days only.

Pick any modelling day to see its raw bar and the ten numbers the models receive. All five models get exactly these columns, on the same rows, with the same target: tomorrow's close minus today's.

The one switch is preprocessing. P0 passes the values as they are. P2 standardises each feature with the training block's mean and spread. Raw, the weekday is about a hundred times larger than a daily return, so it would dominate the distances in SVR and the penalty in ridge. Trees split on one feature at a time and do not care.

Leakage test: append absurd future prices, rebuild the features, and every earlier row must stay identical. Six injected leakage mistakes are all caught by the test suite.

04

Models and tuning

Which models, why each one, and how was each tuned fairly?

Every model is tuned the same way: a random search of 10 settings from a grid fixed before any result, at most 90 s per run, seed 42. Each setting is scored by its mean error over the five training-block folds. The validation block is never used for tuning.

Pick a model to see every trial it ran. Notice the pattern: the winning settings are mostly the most cautious ones, such as the strongest ridge penalty or the smallest boosting learning rate. With almost no signal to fit, the safest forecast stays close to "no change".

Beyond hyperparameters we also explored the target (price level first, then the change, after the level version collapsed toward the training average) and P0 versus P2 scaling. Both tree ensembles gave bit-identical predictions under scaling, exactly as split thresholds predict.

05

Evaluation

Measured how, against what, and how certain?

Each run is compared with persistence, the rule that tomorrow's close equals today's. Four measures, each with a job:

  • MAE: typical miss, in the asset's currency.
  • RMSE: like MAE, but large misses count more.
  • R²: share of price variance explained. High for anything that follows yesterday's price, so it never ranks models here.
  • Skill = 1 − MAEmodel / MAEpersistence. Zero means no better than the rule, and it has no units, so it is the only measure we compare across assets.

The calculator recomputes every measure in your browser from the stored forecasts of the run you pick.

Then uncertainty: errors are paired by date and resampled in blocks of 10 days, 1,000 times, giving a 95% interval for each run's skill.

06

Neural networks experimental

Would a neural network, even one that reads sequences, find what the classical models missed?

The course is about machine learning, so the main comparison stays classical. As a separate, experimental test we asked two neural networks a simpler question: will tomorrow close higher than today?

  • MLP: today's ten features, the same inputs as the main study.
  • LSTM: the last 20 days of those features, read as a sequence. Its inputs differ, so it is not an identical-input comparison.

Settings were chosen on the end of the training block, results averaged over 5 random seeds, and the test block was not read. The yardstick is the majority rule from training: always predict the more common direction.

07

Verdict

How the conclusion follows, one step at a time.

    What students take away

    Before trusting any AI score, ask three things: what could the model see when it predicted, how did a simple rule do on the same examples, and how certain is the improvement? This project answers all three with real data, and the answer to the last one is "not certain at all".
